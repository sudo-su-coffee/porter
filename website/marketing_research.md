# PaaS marketing research notes

## Vercel pattern

Vercel’s current pricing page uses a clear three-tier structure: a free entry point, a paid team-oriented plan, and a custom enterprise tier. The feature lists are compact and outcome-oriented, while calls to action are split between self-serve signup, free trials, and sales conversations. It also makes spend management and included usage visible, which reduces billing ambiguity.

## Fly.io pattern

Fly.io positions itself around usage-based infrastructure with a simple invoice promise, a cost calculator, a strong “Get Started” action, and separate paths for support, compliance, and enterprise. The page’s product story is organized around compute, networking, autoscaling, zero-downtime deploys, routing, and monitoring rather than a generic list of cloud features.

## Porter adaptation

Porter should not copy either visual identity. It should adapt the information architecture: a self-hosted/free path for technical users, a future hosted team tier for convenience and collaboration, and a future enterprise path for managed support and compliance. Until those services exist, the page must label them as planned and use “Join the early access list” or “Read the roadmap” instead of pretending signup or billing is live.

## Content hierarchy for the next revision

The homepage should introduce Porter’s differentiated thesis, show the deployment flow, explain capabilities by outcome, frame the product surface for solo users and teams, add a transparent “now / next” SaaS roadmap, and finish with a strong documentation or early-access conversion. A dedicated Getting Started page should provide prerequisites, the first-run path, configuration, a sample deploy, and troubleshooting.
